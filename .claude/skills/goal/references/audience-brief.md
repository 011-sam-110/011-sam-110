# Audience brief — who this portfolio is for

**Person:** Sam Poplett, 19. CS-with-AI at University of Sussex (Year 1). Takes paid **programming contracts**.
**Goal of the site:** win contract work now, and land an agency role / strong job after uni.
**Primary visitor:** a hiring manager / agency lead / prospective client who has ~60 seconds and a hiring decision to make.

## What this visitor is actually asking
1. *Can this person ship real things?* → proof of work, live demos, code, metrics.
2. *Is the work any good?* → case studies that show thinking, not just screenshots.
3. *Is he current / employable?* → modern stack, AI fluency, active GitHub.
4. *Is he low-risk and easy to work with?* → clear comms, fast contact, availability signalled.
5. *How do I get him?* → an obvious, low-friction way to **book a call** or message.

## Positioning rules (apply to all copy & layout)
- **Lead with outcomes, not adjectives.** "Built an AML compliance auditor scoring risk 0–100 with a blockchain audit
  trail" beats "passionate developer". Numbers > claims.
- **Show, then tell.** Every project links to code/demo. Case studies follow problem → approach → architecture → result.
- **Signal availability.** An "Open to contracts / grad roles" status + a **Book a call** CTA above the fold and in
  the contact section. Reduce the steps between "interested" and "talking to Sam" to one click.
- **Lean into the AI-engineer angle** — it's his edge as a junior. Agentic systems, vision pipelines, LLM determinism.
- **Honesty bar.** He's a first-year with real hackathon wins and shipped projects. Don't invent jobs, titles, or
  fake testimonials. Aspirational framing is fine; fabricated credentials are not.
- **Don't over-design.** Motion and 3D must serve credibility (feels crafted, performant), never bury the content or
  slow the first paint. A janky 3D hero is worse than no 3D hero.

## Tone
Confident, concrete, a little understated. Builder energy. No buzzword soup, no "ninja/rockstar".
